# Anti-Money Laundering and Counter-Terrorism Financing Act 2006 (National) — Operational Summary

> ORIGINAL SUMMARY. Paraphrased operational guidance on the *Anti-Money Laundering and
> Counter-Terrorism Financing Act 2006* (Cth) as amended by the *AML/CTF Amendment Act 2024*,
> reflecting reforms in effect from **31 March 2026**. Not the verbatim statute. Applies in
> all states (venue_state = null). Federal regime administered by **AUSTRAC**.
> DEPLOY FLAG: confirm thresholds, dates, and mechanics with a licensed Australian solicitor
> and load the full consolidated statute + AUSTRAC rules before production sign-off.
>
> SCOPE NOTE: this is the **Commonwealth AML/CTF** regime. It is distinct from the QLD
> Gaming Machine Act / Responsible Gambling Code "prescribed threshold" for paying winnings
> by cheque (a gambling-harm-minimisation rule). See "Boundary — not the QLD pay-by-cheque
> threshold" below. Do not conflate the two.

## Who is captured (reporting entities)
Pubs and clubs that operate gaming machines provide a **designated service** under the
AML/CTF Act and are **reporting entities**. As reporting entities they must enrol with
AUSTRAC, maintain an AML/CTF program, carry out customer due diligence, keep records, and
make the required reports. Mise's gaming-pub clients are within this captured group.

## Customer due diligence (CDD) — $5,000 gaming-payout threshold
From **31 March 2026** the customer due-diligence threshold for gaming-related cash
transactions (including gaming payouts) was **reduced from $10,000 to $5,000**. At or above
**$5,000**, the venue must carry out customer due diligence — establishing and verifying the
customer's identity on reasonable grounds — before or at the time the relevant gaming
transaction is provided. The previous $10,000 figure is superseded and must not be cited as
current.

## Single consolidated AML/CTF program (Part A / Part B abolished)
From 31 March 2026, AML/CTF programs are consolidated into a **single program**. The former
split between a **Part A** (money-laundering / terrorism-financing risk assessment and
management) and a **Part B** (customer identification / CDD procedures) has been **abolished**.
A reporting entity now maintains one consolidated AML/CTF program that covers both its ML/TF
risk assessment and its customer-facing due-diligence policies and procedures.

## AML/CTF compliance officer and AUSTRAC notification
A reporting entity must designate an **AML/CTF compliance officer** and notify AUSTRAC of that
appointment. For entities already enrolled, the notification was due by **30 May 2026**. The
compliance officer is responsible for oversight of the consolidated AML/CTF program.

## Ongoing reporting obligations
Reporting entities must continue to submit **threshold transaction reports** for cash
transactions at or above the reporting threshold, **suspicious matter reports** where there are
reasonable grounds to suspect money laundering or terrorism financing, and to keep prescribed
records. These reporting duties operate in addition to the CDD trigger above.

## Boundary — not the QLD pay-by-cheque "prescribed threshold"
The **$5,000 AML/CTF CDD trigger is a federal customer-identification obligation** and is
**separate** from the Queensland *Gaming Machine Act 1991* / *Responsible Gambling Code of
Practice* requirement that winnings at or above a *prescribed threshold* be paid by cheque or
electronic transfer rather than cash. The QLD rule governs **how winnings are paid out** to
reduce gambling harm; the AML/CTF rule governs **when a venue must identify the customer** to
counter money laundering. A single transaction can engage both, but they are different
thresholds under different laws and must not be merged in any answer.

## Forward note — Tranche 2 (not yet in force)
A further "Tranche 2" of newly-designated services is scheduled to commence **1 July 2026**
(with enrolment by 29 July 2026). This is **not yet in effect** as of this summary and should
be presented as forthcoming, not current, until it commences.
