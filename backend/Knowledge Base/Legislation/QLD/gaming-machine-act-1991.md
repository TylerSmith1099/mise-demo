# Gaming Machine Act 1991 (QLD) — Operational Summary

> ORIGINAL SUMMARY. Paraphrased operational guidance covering the effect of key
> provisions of the Gaming Machine Act 1991 (Qld). Not the verbatim statute.
> DEPLOY FLAG: load the full consolidated statute text from the Queensland
> legislation register before production sign-off.

## Licensed hours and operation of gaming machines
Gaming machines may only be operated during the venue's approved gaming hours as
fixed by the gaming machine licence. A licensee must not permit a gaming machine
to be played outside approved hours. Any approved variation to gaming hours must
be displayed and adhered to. If a machine malfunctions during approved hours it
must be locked out and not returned to play until the fault is cleared and, where
required, the result of any affected game is resolved in the player's favour or
the credit is preserved.

## Gaming machine malfunctions and disputed results
Where a gaming machine malfunctions, any indicated win or credit produced solely
by the malfunction is void. Staff must record the malfunction, lock the machine
out of play, and report it to the licensed monitoring operator and, where the
fault affects metering or payout, to the regulator. A genuine win that is not the
product of a malfunction must be paid. Disputes about a result are escalated to
the venue's nominee or duty manager, who records the dispute and the resolution.

## Minors must not enter the gaming machine area
A person under 18 must not enter or remain in a designated gaming machine area. A
licensee and staff must take reasonable steps to ensure minors do not enter the
area, including checking acceptable photo identification where a person may be
under 25. A minor found in the area must be asked to leave immediately and the
incident recorded.

## Cash facilities and payment of winnings
Cash dispensing facilities (ATMs) must not be located in the gaming machine area,
and there are limits on cash that may be provided. Winnings at or above the
prescribed threshold must be paid wholly or partly by cheque or electronic
transfer rather than cash, and a record kept. Staff must not provide credit for
gaming or accept the redemption of a cheque drawn to pay winnings.

## Self-exclusion and excluded persons
A licensee must comply with exclusion directions. Where a person is the subject of
a self-exclusion or a venue exclusion, staff must take reasonable steps to prevent
that person from gambling and, where identified in the gaming area, ask them to
leave. Re-entry of an excluded person is recorded and reported as required.

## Record keeping and reporting to the regulator
The licensee must keep accurate records of gaming machine turnover, metered data,
clearances, and malfunctions, and make them available to the regulator on request.
Significant incidents — major malfunctions, suspected tampering, and exclusion
breaches — must be reported within the required timeframe.
